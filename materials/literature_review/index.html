Step 1: Global literature and precedents (2023-2026)

How aviation changes translate into hotel demand at transit hubs, and what we adapted for Abu Dhabi.

How this review was produced

  1. Web and scholarly search (OpenAlex, arXiv, publisher pages). Every DOI below was resolved.
  2. A NotebookLM notebook (research/notebooklm/run_research.sh, reproducible) seeded with 13 verified sources, extended by Deep Research to 83 sources, then queried with six targeted questions. The answers are in research/notebooklm/out/q*.md and the briefing in out/briefing.md.
  3. Caveat. Two NotebookLM "sources" are reports written by its own Deep Research agent ("Econometric Framework for Converting Airline Seat Capacity..." and "Quantifying Aviation-Driven Hospitality Elasticities..."). We do not cite numbers from them. The NotebookLM briefing also drifted into generic gravity-model material, so we treated it as idea generation, not evidence.

Gap we fill. We found no 2023-2026 peer-reviewed study that links flight changes to hotel guests by source market at a Gulf hub, or that models disruption-driven hotel demand by distance from the airport.

Three methodologies adapted for Abu Dhabi

1. Seat-elasticity conversion chain with an anchored ML correction (sources 1, 2, 5, 6)

  • What it is. A transparent cascade: seats x load factor x (1 - transfer share) x conversion x origin-to-nationality mix, plus non-air "ground" guests, then x ALOS. A gradient-boosted model corrects what fixed ratios miss.
  • Adaptation:
  • Estimate the conversion per origin market and the ground guests by bounded least squares.
  • Anchor each forecast on the same month last year, scaled by the chain's predicted change. A COVID or closure month is never used as the base.
  • Learn the residual with CatBoost, using source-market holidays, Ramadan/Eid (Hijri calendar), events, weather and supply pressure.
  • Pass the filed schedule as a known-future input.
  • Result (synthetic back-test, rolling origin): WMAPE 6.5% by market and month (4.0% on the emirate total), against 10.4% for seasonal naive and 14.7% for Prophet. Through the 2026 closure: 7.9% vs 12.0% for seasonal naive.

2. Disruption hurdle model with gravity allocation and conformal ranges (sources 8, 9, 11)

  • What it is. Flight-level P(surge) and expected rooms, from information an operations centre has in real time: load, transfer share, delay or cancellation, arrival hour, network capacity. Rooms are split across hotel clusters with share ~ spare rooms x exp(-0.12 km), and the room count comes with a split-conformal 90% interval.
  • Adaptation: calibrated on the 2026 closure magnitudes and the Southwest surge.
  • Result: on normal days, AUC 0.99 and daily distressed-room WMAPE 11%. It is honest about failure: it does not extrapolate to closure days. There the simulator switches to the rule-based replay.

3. Causal shock and counterfactual evaluation (sources 3, 4, 10, 11)

  • What it is. Treat route launches, route losses and the 2026 closure as natural experiments, and compare against a counterfactual.
  • Adaptation: the synthetic world is generated twice with identical random draws, with and without the Feb-2026 closure. Their difference is the causal effect.
  • Applies to:
  • "One shock, two signs": lost inbound guests vs captive stranded nights.
  • Route-loss scenarios, checked against the -34% DiD benchmark.
  • With DCT data: synthetic DiD on real route events.

Novel angles in the prototype

  1. Anchored chain. History carries the level and the chain carries the change, so the simulator stays explainable and still beats naive and ML-only baselines.
  2. Transfer share as the top aviation lever. The Sobol analysis shows a 5-point change in transfer share moves guest nights more than a 10% change in seats. The policy hook is stopover conversion.
  3. One shock, two signs. The 2026 closure removed inbound guests and created captive stranded nights at the same time. The simulator separates the two.
  4. Disruption-to-hotel early warning. The "300 passengers delayed past midnight" question becomes a probability with a room range and a cluster split.
  5. Data-catch finding. The origin-to-nationality mix cannot be learned reliably from monthly aggregates (tested). It needs a prior, so we recommend that DCT collect a periodic sample linking arrival flight origin to nationality.
