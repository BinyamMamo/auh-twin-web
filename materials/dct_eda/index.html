  • Guests are an in-house count, a stock. New Arrivals are check-ins, a flow, and are a subset of Guests on 99.2% of rows.
  • 238 nationality-days are missing from train. The model completes the daily grid and never scores those days.

Findings

  1. Guests are a stock fed by arrivals. Guests divided by arrivals averages 3.7, close to the mean stay in nights. A 2-parameter stock-flow line fits the international total to 3.2% WMAPE, but only when yesterday's actual guests are known. The test period gives arrivals, not guests. The useful model is therefore a stay curve: today's guests as non-negative weights on the last 28 days of arrivals.
  2. Stay length differs by a factor of three across nationalities.
  3. Shortest: Oman 1.6, Qatar 2.1, China 2.3, Saudi Arabia 2.5, domestic 2.5.
  4. Longest: Germany, Russia, Egypt, Armenia, Finland, Sweden and the UK, at 4.9 to 5.1.
  5. Any lever that shifts the nationality mix shifts guest nights more than the passenger count alone suggests.
  6. International demand doubled while domestic shrank.
  7. International: 13.0k guests a day in 2022, 18.2k in 2023, 23.5k in 2024, 25.6k in 2025 to July.
  8. Domestic: 19.3k, 18.5k, 16.6k, 15.6k.
  9. Because of this trend, models fitted on the most recent 400 days beat models fitted on all history.
  10. The calendar shapes are opposite.
  11. International peaks in Oct to Feb (Dec index 1.16) and dips in Jun and Sep (0.82 and 0.83).
  12. Domestic peaks in summer and December (Jul 1.07, Aug 1.09, Dec 1.08).
  13. Domestic is a weekend market: Friday 1.08, Saturday 1.16, Sunday 0.89. International barely moves by weekday.
  14. Origin is not nationality. This is the brief's data catch, and it shows clearly.
  15. Twelve nationalities have no direct flight in the data: Pakistan, Australia, Brazil, Mexico, Morocco, South Africa, Czechia, Romania and the four Nordic countries. Their guests arrive through other hubs or by road.
  16. Hotel arrivals per point-to-point passenger from the same country vary widely:
    • China 4.7, Canada 2.4 and USA 2.1: most of these guests fly in on connections, not the direct route;
    • India 0.17, Egypt 0.12 and Turkey 0.12: most direct passengers are not hotel guests (residents, workers, visiting family);
    • the UK, Germany, Belgium and Ireland are close to 1.0.
  17. The GCC neighbours drive. Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain and Oman have the weakest daily link between flights and arrivals.
  18. About half of arriving passengers fly onward. The point-to-point share fell from 52.3% in 2023 to 49.6% in 2025 and 47.7% in early 2026. Every point of transfer share converted to stopover is hotel demand.
  19. The test window ends on a shock. On 28 Feb 2026, the start of the Gulf airspace closure, seats fell from about 39k a day to 22.5k and P2P passengers from 13.2k to 7.9k.

Consequences for the model

  • Submission (nowcast). Predict guests from known arrivals with a per-nationality stay curve, then test whether machine learning or foundation models add anything. See dct_backtest.md.
  • Simulator (what-if). Flights to arrivals is the weak link: about 35% daily WMAPE for arrivals from P2P alone. It is much better for markets that fly direct (UK, Germany, Russia) than for drive-in GCC markets or China. The simulator should state that uncertainty openly rather than hide it.
