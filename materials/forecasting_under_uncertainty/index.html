Predicting when the world is unpredictable: what AUH Twin should borrow

Research note, 7 Oct 2026, branch research-agents. It answers three questions: - Should live news (for example through an agent like Hermes) feed the prediction? - Should a MiroFish-style swarm of LLM agents, or another multi-agent or digital-twin simulation, help predict? - What do people in fields with no formula (stocks, prediction markets, geopolitics) actually do, and what transfers to our challenge?

The judging weights frame every answer: 40% back-tested accuracy (WMAPE) against history, and 20% each for creativity, practicality and clarity.

Short answer

  • Do not put live news or LLM judgement into the scored prediction. It cannot be back-tested without leaking the future, and it would put the 40% accuracy score at risk.
  • Do not use MiroFish. Its output is narrative, it has no accuracy record, its license is AGPL, and our data is aggregated, so its agents would have nothing real to be calibrated against.
  • Do borrow the habits that work in unpredictable domains:
  • start from base rates;
  • notice when the world has changed;
  • plan with scenarios instead of point guesses;
  • judge decisions by how they hold up across many futures.
  • Our own data shows where this pays: the simulator's biggest misses come from anchoring on a shocked month and from moving holidays. Both can be fixed without any outside data.

How others forecast the unforecastable

Geopolitical forecasting (Tetlock, Good Judgment Project) Start from base rates, break the question into parts, update in small steps on news, aggregate many forecasters weighted by track record Beat the control group by more than 60% and an intelligence-community prediction market by 25 to 30% (AI Impacts summary, Collins on Superforecasting)
Prediction markets (Polymarket, Kalshi) Prices pool dispersed information; their real value is calibration, not certainty 188,509 resolved markets: well calibrated overall, underconfident in politics; about 3% of traders take about 30% of gains (arXiv 2602.19520, LessWrong calibration study)

The common thread. The people who do well in unpredictable domains don't predict the shock. They: 1. combine forecasts; 2. check their calibration; 3. start from what usually happens; 4. notice quickly when the world has changed; 5. choose actions that hold up whichever future arrives.

AUH Twin already does items 1 and 2: the median-of-4 ensemble, the back-tested ranges and the pre-registered rules. Items 3 to 5 are the gap.

Question 1: live news through an agent like Hermes

Hermes Agent (Nous Research, MIT) is a persistent, self-improving agent with 70+ tools. It is excellent at gathering and summarising information; it is not a forecasting model.

Verdict: not in the scored model; yes as a watch layer. - The scored model can't be back-tested honestly. An LLM-plus-search pipeline asked about 2024 months already "knows" what happened, and nothing we could report would prove otherwise. Judges weight that number at 40%. - It isn't deterministic, and it needs the network at the booth. - A watch layer works. An agent reads current headlines (an airspace notice, a travel advisory, a conflict) and proposes a shock scenario in the simulator ("UAE to Israel flights suspended for 60 days, L-shaped recovery"). A planner accepts or rejects it. That is creative and practical, and it never touches the validated numbers. - Any news signal that enters the model must be timestamped data. GDELT records world news events and tone back to 2015, fixed at publication time, so it can be back-tested without leakage. In our checks the live API was reachable but throttled (one query every 5 seconds per IP), so a proper covariate needs GDELT's bulk daily files. Worth a pre-registered challenger after the deadline, not before.

Question 2: MiroFish, multi-agent swarms or a digital twin

MiroFish turns a document into a knowledge graph and spawns thousands of persona agents. They post and debate on a simulated social network built on CAMEL-AI's OASIS, and their interactions are read as a forecast.

Verdict: no. - Its output is narrative, not a calibrated number. We found no published back-tests. - It costs thousands of LLM calls per run and isn't reproducible. - Its AGPL-3.0 license would cover our distributed code. - Our data is aggregated (nationality by day), and the brief forbids passenger-level data, so persona agents would have nothing real to be calibrated against.

What we recommend instead: we already have the digital twin. The simulator is an aggregate twin with back-tested transitions (seats to guest nights). The better-planned agent idea is an analyst agent that uses the validated simulator as its tool, following RAND's robust decision making: - it scans every candidate new route and frequency change; - it runs each one across many futures (lever uncertainty plus back-tested error); - it ranks the options by the guest nights you can count on at the 10th percentile, not just the average; - it explains the top picks in plain words through the existing Laya and Gemini Ask path.

Every number still comes from the back-tested engine, and it answers the brief's own question, "which countries bring the most hotel guests per available seat", as a decision tool.

What our data says (feasibility checks)

Script: research/scripts/feasibility.py (read-only, DCT data).

  • There's enough history for a reference class of new routes.
  • Since April 2023 there have been 124 route launches (city x airline), 14 of them material, adding more than 15% to a country's seats.
  • For 11 of those, guest nights in the three months after launch moved by a median of about +35% against a year earlier: Paris +20%, Osaka +22%, Rome +14% to +35%, Baku +142%, Shanghai -15%.
  • Overall international growth was about +30% a year, so the distribution is wide but real.
  • It can replace today's assumed "median capture" for a route with no history.
  • Shocks are visible.
  • Israeli guest nights fell 67%, 89% and 83% year on year in Oct, Nov and Dec 2023, then recovered within about a year (+66% by Oct 2024).
  • Russian guest nights rose 290% in March 2023 as travel rerouted after sanctions.
  • The 28 Feb 2026 airspace closure cut seats into AUH by about 42% in a day.
  • The simulator's biggest misses have two fixable causes.
  • Anchoring on a shocked month. Israel in Nov 2024 came in at 3.2 times the prediction, because the anchor was the war-hit Nov 2023. Qatar and Azerbaijan in 2023 show similar jumps.
  • Moving holidays. China in Jan 2025 came in at 3.0 times the prediction: Lunar New Year was on 29 Jan 2025 but 10 Feb 2024, so January was compared with a non-holiday January.

What we would tell the judges

"Nobody can predict the next war or closure, and we don't pretend to. We did what forecasters do in unpredictable fields: - we combine models and publish calibrated ranges; - we start from what usually happens; - we notice when the world has changed; - we stress-test decisions across futures.

The simulator tells a planner which routes still pay off when things go wrong."
